This amendment is frozen before outcome computation. It resolves an ambiguity between the parent preregistration's phrase **“a later bar close”** and Amendment 01's update-before-test implementation language.

## Strict later-bar confirmation rule

A candidate extreme formed on detector bar `t` may **not** be confirmed as a terminal endpoint by the close of the same bar `t`.

For an up leg:

1. At bar `t`, first test whether the close of `t` confirms the running-high candidate established on a strictly earlier bar.
2. Only after that test, if no confirmation occurred, allow the high of `t` to replace the running-high candidate.
3. A new high made on `t` therefore cannot become a confirmed terminal high until bar `t+1` or later.

For a down leg the logic is symmetric: the close of `t` may confirm only a running-low candidate from a strictly earlier bar; the low of `t` is adopted as a new candidate only after the confirmation test.

Neutral initialization follows the same strict ordering: a bar close may confirm only an opposite extreme candidate established before that bar; any new extreme on the current bar is stored only after the confirmation test.

If a prior candidate is confirmed on bar `t`, state flips and bar `t` supplies the initial candidate extreme for the new leg, but that new candidate cannot itself be confirmed until a later bar.

## Why this clarification is required

The follow-on wick study measures rejection geometry on the candidate endpoint candle. If same-bar confirmation were permitted, a large directional wick could mechanically contribute to the close-to-extreme ATR reversal condition that creates the terminal label. Requiring strictly later confirmation preserves the intended separation:

- terminal identity is generated by later price action and the pivot-blind ATR directional-change process;
- wick geometry is observable at the candidate candle close;
- wick geometry is not mechanically used by that same close to certify itself as terminal.

This amendment supersedes only the update-before-test ordering language in Amendment 01. All thresholds, horizons, spatial geometry, inference rules, gates and holdout restrictions remain unchanged.
